## 🛰️ Allaneus from Orbit

From a ship coming in, Allaneus is white: ice, frozen stone and glacier, from pole to pole. Then you see the green.

Scattered across the white are thousands of green spots, like moss on a cold wall. These are **the Greens**: places where a heat vent breaks the surface and life has gathered round it. Most of them lie in a belt around the equator. The rest run across the world in long dotted lines, following **the Seams**, the cracks where the great plates of the world's crust grind against each other.

Where two Seams cross, on the equator, is the biggest green of all: a ring of mountains with a dark, steaming heart, nearly a hundred kilometres across. That's **the Rim**, and it's where the Society lives.

## 🔥 The Vents

Allaneus is frozen on top and molten underneath. A **vent** is anywhere the heat breaks through: a crack you could step over, a steaming pool, or a shaft tens of kilometres wide that drops further than anyone has ever measured.

A vent brings up three things, and life needs all three:

| What | Why it matters |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🔥 **Heat** | Out on the Pale, the cold kills in an hour. Near a vent, the ground is warm and the air is soft |
| 🌬️ **Breath** | The gases a vent breathes out are poison on their own. The life around the vent turns them into air people can breathe |
| ⛏️ **Minerals** | Salts, sulphur, coal, iron, copper and stranger things, pushed up from below and left in the walls. Every seam the Works has ever mined came up this way |

**Away from a vent, the air goes thin.** You can breathe on the Pale, for a while, but every breath is cold and not quite enough. Haulers and anyone else who goes far from the Rim wear a **lung**: a mask packed with damp breathmoss, which keeps making air as long as you keep it warm and wet.

## 🌿 Life at the Vents

Every living thing native to Allaneus started at a vent mouth, and none of it strays far.

| Life | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🟢 **Breathmoss** | Thick red-green moss that coats the throats of the vents and makes the air. Every green spot you can see from orbit is mostly breathmoss. Burning it is a crime in the Heights and a sin in the Works. The Works calls it **the Mother's breath** |
| 🌲 **Ventwood** | Black-barked trees with roots that grow down towards the heat. The forests of the Crust are ventwood. The wood is dense, slow to burn and almost impossible to cut, so only the Heights can afford it |
| 🍃 **Bitterleaf** | A low grey shrub that grows anywhere the ground is warm, including on pipes. Boiled for **pump tea** |
| 🍄 **Cellar-cap** | A pale mushroom that grows in warm dark places. Every Works basement has a crop |
| 🦋 **Glasswings** | Small clear-winged flyers that ride the updrafts over the vents in swarms. When the glasswings stop flying over a vent, something is wrong with it |
| 🦀 **Scuttlers** | Hard-shelled vent crabs the size of a hand, living in the warm cracks of the walls. Fried on a boiler-top, they're the Works' favourite treat after fried dough |
| 🐍 **Blind eels** | Long pale eels that live in the Sunk's bitterwater. Edible, if you know how to cook them, and the ferrymen do |
| 👁️ **Grims** | Pale, many-legged scavengers from somewhere below the Sunk's lamps. Ferrymen say they've seen them. Most people say ferrymen say a lot of things |

**What the Charter brought.** People, wheat, rats and pigeons came with the first ships, along with the roses, oaks and fruit trees in the Heights' glasshouses. In the Heights this is called **old stock**, and it's a mark of breeding. Ventwood furniture is for new money.

## 🌋 The Rim

The Rim is the mouth of a super volcano so old it has no name: a caldera nearly a hundred kilometres across, which crusted over long ago.

- **The Rimwall** is the broken ring of mountains around it. Its peaks are cold, bare and lashed by the wind off the Pale. The Skyquay sits up there, where ships can land without scorching anything that matters, and so does the **Icegate**, where the ice roads come in.
- **The Crust** is the caldera's floor: a plain of old cooled rock, warm underfoot, steaming where it cracks. It looks solid, and mostly it is. Atop the Crust is the top of the Society: **the Heights**, with its estates, its **ventwood forests**, Aurel's **fields** of grain, and the great glasshouses of **the Conservatory**, where the newest Refinement is grown.
- **The vents** punch through the Crust in dozens of places, and beneath every one of them, down the walls and through the tunnels, lives everyone who isn't in the Heights.

From the top of the Rimwall you can see the whole Society at once: green on top, steam rising out of the holes, and somewhere underneath, two kilometres down, your district.

Around **five million people** live in the Rim. Two million of them live in the Well. Fewer than forty thousand live in the Heights.

## 🕳️ The Wells of the Rim

Any vent big enough to build a city down is called a **well**. Most of the Rim's vents are too small, too hot or too new. These are the ones that matter.

| Well | Where | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 💧 **The Well** | The centre of the Rim | The Charter vent: about twelve kilometres across, its walls dropping two kilometres to the **Floor**. The Heights line its edge, **the Brink**. Below are the Terraces, the Span, the Works and the Kindling. Most of the Society lives here, and so does your crew |
| 🔴 **The Maw** | North of the Well | The biggest vent in the Rim, nearly twenty kilometres across. Too hot to live in, and no bottom anyone has found. It glows red at night, and its breath feeds the thickest forests on the Crust. The Works calls it **the Mother's Mouth** |
| ⚫ **Cinderwell** | West of the Well | Varre's well, six kilometres across, its walls black with coal and red with iron. Foundry districts all the way down, joined to Slagside by the **Slag Bore** |
| 🌾 **The Cradle** | South of the Well | Aurel's well: wide, shallow and gentle, its warm walls terraced with vat-farms and glass. Tallow Steps is here. The only Works districts that smell of bread |
| 🌲 **The Hollow** | North-west of the Well | A well that went cold and crusted over in the 240s AC, in less than a year. It was House Hollin's. Now there's a ventwood forest on top that nobody builds in, and the Hollow's old Terraces are sealed |
| 💨 **The Smokes** | All over the Crust | Hundreds of small vents, too small to build in. The houses use them as chimneys, hot baths and rubbish pits |
| 🆕 **The Newmouth** | In Aurel's fields, south-east | A new vent that opened in a shudder in 405 AC. The Keepers fenced it off within a day, and nobody else has been allowed near it since |

## 🚇 The Bores

The Bonded didn't only dig down. They dug across. Tunnels called **bores** join the wells of the Rim to each other, and every bore runs at the depth of one layer of the Society.

| Layer | Its bores | What they're like |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ✨ **The Heights** | None | The Heights travel on top, across the Crust, by carriage and by the **Quay Line**, the steam railway to the Skyquay |
| 🎩 **The Terraces** | **The Galleries** | Tiled, lamplit and gated at both ends. They join the Terraces of every living well, so a steward can go from Tidehouse's service floors to the Cradle without ever seeing anyone from below |
| 🤝 **The Margin** | **The Crossings** | Where all the bores meet. The Crossings join the Span to every other well, and the Margin Peace holds in them as it does on the Span |
| 🔩 **The Works** | **The Runs** | Great freight bores with steam rail down the middle, carrying coal, ore, grain and people between the Works of every well. Raiders use them too |
| 🛶 **The Sunk** | **The old bores** | The Bonded's first tunnels: mine galleries, flooded now. Some lead to wells nobody lives in any more |
| 🕳️ **Below** | **The Deep** | No bores. Below the Floor of every well, the vent goes on. Nobody has found the bottom |

**Sideways, never up.** The bores join wells at the same depth. A Works family can walk to the Works of another well without passing a single gate. But going up a layer always means a wall, a stair, a Hoist or a Lockgate. Works folk will tell you that's not an accident. The Charter Houses drew the plans.

## 🫨 Shudders

The Rim is a sleeping volcano, and it moves in its sleep.

Small **shudders** come every few weeks: a rattle in the cups, a ticking in the pipes, the Weep shaken loose from the Murk. Big ones come every few years. They crack pressure lines, close old bores, open new seams in the walls, and once in a long while they open a new vent or let an old one go cold.

- **Seam-cutters love them.** A big shudder pushes fresh ore into the walls, and the first crew to find it eats well for a year.
- **Gaugers hate them.** A shudder is the easiest way for pressure to drop, and the hardest to prove it wasn't someone's fault.
- **The Keepers measure them.** They keep **listening posts** all over the Crust, little domes of living glass that hum when the ground moves. They have never published what they hear.

When the ground moves, Works folk put a hand flat on the floor and say ***"Easy, Mother."***

## 🧊 The Pale and the Greens

Beyond the Rimwall is **the Pale**: hundreds of kilometres of frozen stone and glacier, under a white sky. The wind never stops. The cold kills in an hour, and the thin air takes longer, but it gets there.

The Pale isn't empty. Along the Seams lie **the Greens**: other vents, small and wild, each a patch of breathmoss, ventwood and steam in the middle of the ice. Most are a few hundred metres across. None is big enough to build a city in.

- **The ice roads** follow the Seams from Green to Green, because a crawler crew needs somewhere to breathe, warm up and fix an engine. The haulers call these stops **wayvents**. The **Shelter at Nine Mile** sits on the last of them before the glaciers.
- **The Unsworn.** Some Greens have people on them: families who left the Society and went out onto the Pale. The houses say nobody lives out there. The haulers trade with them, and say nothing.
- **The far Greens.** Thousands more lie along the equator, weeks or months away by crawler. Nobody from the Rim has been to most of them. Ships' crews say you can see them from orbit, glowing faintly at night.

## 🌙 The Three Moons

Three moons circle Allaneus, and from the Crust at least one is always in the sky, day and night. From the Works, you can't see any of them.

| Moon | Works name | What it looks like | What's done there |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ⚪ **The Salt** | **The Loaf** (round, white and out of reach) | The biggest: pale grey-white, with dark plains on its face, like the moon of old Earth in the stories | **Crown mines.** The metals for the chip alloy and the Conservatory's glass come from the Salt. The miners are contract crews, sworn to the Crown for ten years |
| 🔴 **The Ember** | **The Coal** | Bright rust red, smaller than the Salt, with a faint orange speck on its dark side: the lamps of the mines | **The Ember Lease.** Varre's iron and rare-ore mines, held by writ since 129 AC. Since 381 AC every load of Varre's ore has come down on Antioch's ships, and Varre has paid Antioch for the privilege |
| ⚪ **The Shard** | **The Sliver** | Small, white and lumpy, low and fast. It crosses the sky twice a day | **The Netwrights' station**, where the Clean Sky's nets are mended and emptied. Also the **Shardworks**, Corvane's penal mine. People sent to the Shardworks are said to be **sent up the Shard**. Some come back |

**Three Lamps.** Once every few years all three moons are full and up at the same time. The Heights call it **Three Lamps** and hold weddings under it. The Works says *"Three lamps, no shadows"*, and no crew works that night if it can help it.

## ✨ The Clean Sky

The sky over Allaneus is almost empty, and the law keeps it that way.

The oldest story in the Ascendancy is about **Earth**, the first world, where people began. Earth filled its own sky with broken machines and rubbish until the rubbish began to hit the ships, and for a long, terrible time nobody could leave. Every world of the Ascendancy has been bound since by **the Clean Sky**, a law older than the Charter: whatever goes up must come down, or be caught.

- **The Nets.** Great nets of grown filament, kilometres wide, swing round Allaneus on long slow paths, catching anything left in orbit: a lost bolt, a dead engine, a frozen body. The **Netwrights** of the Shard empty them and bring the catch down to the Skyquay.
- **The Sweep.** When a Net crosses the sky in sunlight, it looks like a swarm of shooting stars moving the wrong way, slow and glittering. That's **the Sweep**. Children are told to make a wish on it. In the Heights the wish is usually spoken. In the Works it's usually not.
- **Sweep Night.** Once a year the biggest Net, **the Great Net**, crosses straight over the Rim at night. See [Belief and Custom](world_customs.md#-the-year).
- **Who answers for it.** The Master of the Fleet keeps the Clean Sky over Allaneus. A ship that leaves anything in orbit forfeits its cargo, and its captain never lands on Allaneus again.
- **What the Nets catch.** Mostly rubbish. The catch is auctioned at the Skyquay, and the yards buy it for scrap. Now and then something comes down that isn't rubbish, and the auction is cancelled.

There are almost no other made things in the sky. The Monarch's ships don't wait in orbit: they come straight down to the Skyquay and leave as soon as they're loaded.
