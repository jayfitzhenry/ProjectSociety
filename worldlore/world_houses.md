## 🎭 How the Heights Works

The Heights is the top of **the Society**, which is what the houses call the whole ordered stack of the Rim: themselves on the Crust, their servants in the Terraces, and everyone else below, each layer in its place. The Heights runs on three things: **Seats**, **face** and **the Season**.

- **Seats** are power. A Seat is an office granted by the Monarch that lets a house run part of the city and take a share of everything that passes through it. A house with no Seat lives on its estates and its districts, and its neighbours start to wonder how long it'll last. See [The Seats](#-the-seats).
- **Face** is reputation, and in the Heights it's worth more than chips. A house that's seen to lose (a cargo stolen, a daughter's engagement broken, a crew caught in its colours) loses face, and a house that loses enough face loses its allies, then its Seats. Nobles will pay almost anything to avoid looking foolish. That's why they need crews: a crew can do the ugly thing, and the house can look surprised.
- **The Season** is the round of balls, races, salons and weddings that runs from the first frost on the Pale to the thaw. Every alliance in the Heights is made or broken during the Season. It's also when crews are busiest: everyone who matters is out of their house at the same time, and every one of them has a secret in their rooms.

### What nobles are like

Works folk call them **stiffs**, because of how still they stand. Grafted young, nobles heal clean, live long and rarely look their age, and they move as if nothing in the world could hurry them. They're polite to everyone, including the people they're ruining. They almost never raise their voices, because they don't need to.

Most nobles have never been below the Terraces. Many have never been below the Hoists. Their world is the Crust: estates, hunting forests of ventwood, glasshouses and the long carriage roads between the wells. To a noble, the Works is weather: something that happens to other people, makes noise and occasionally needs managing.

They are not stupid. A noble house has survived four hundred years of the Heights by being cleverer, more patient and more ruthless than its neighbours. Treat one like a fool and you'll find out.

## 👑 The Crown

Above the houses is the Monarch, and the Monarch's institutions on Allaneus belong to no house at all.

### The Monarch

The Monarch rules the Ascendancy from the **Throne-world**, many months away by ship. No Monarch has ever visited Allaneus. Whether there has been one Monarch for four hundred years or many, nobody in the Works could tell you, and nobody in the Heights will. The Monarch's face on the chips has never changed.

The Monarch is the law. In practice that means **the writ**: a sealed order from the Throne-world, carried by ship, that grants a Seat, raises a house, ends a war, or does anything else the Monarch wishes. Nobody on Allaneus can overturn a writ. The houses spend a great deal of money making sure the right ones get written.

### The Conservatory

The Crown's great glasshouses of living glass on the Crust beside the Well, built over a small vent of their own, where all Refinement on Allaneus is grown. Its **Keepers** wear plain green, take vows to the Monarch, give up their family names, and serve every house equally, for a price. The Conservatory holds the **seed-stock** that every Refined thing starts from. Nobody outside it knows how much it holds or where it came from. See [Refinement](lore.md#-refinement).

### The Crown Mint

Beside the Conservatory, and run by its Keepers. The Mint grows chips from metal mined on **the Salt**, the great white moon, which belongs to the Crown alone. Every chip in the Works came from here, and the Mint knows how many it has grown. Every house is suspected of wanting its own mint, and every house denies it.

### The Netwrights

The Crown's crews in orbit, based on **the Shard**, the smallest moon. They tend the great **Nets** that keep the sky of Allaneus clean, and bring the catch down to the Skyquay. The Master of the Fleet answers for the Clean Sky, so in practice the Netwrights take their orders from Antioch, and every other house resents it. See [The Clean Sky](world_rim.md#-the-clean-sky).

### The Assessor

Every few decades the Monarch sends an **Assessor** to Allaneus to judge whether each house deserves its Seats. The Assessor's word is the Monarch's word. The last one came in **348 AC**, and three years later a family of ice-haulers was made a noble house. One is coming again this year. See [This Year](#-this-year-the-assessment).

## 🪑 The Seats

| Seat | What it runs | Held by | Who wants it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 👑 **Governor of Allaneus** | The city, the Great Pumps, disputes between houses, and the Governor's share of every tax | **Antioch** (since 397 AC) | Everyone |
| ⚓ **Master of the Fleet** | The Skyquay, the fleet yards, every ship and cargo leaving the world or coming down from the moons, and the Clean Sky | **Antioch** (since 381 AC) | **Varre**, who held it for a century |
| ⚖️ **Warden of the Well** | The Greycoats, the Lockgates, the courts, the Drop and the Shardworks | **Corvane** (always) | Nobody says so out loud |
| 🌾 **Steward of the Granaries** | The Sowings, grain lifts, vat-farms, ration boards | **Aurel** (always) | Lysander, quietly |
| 🕯️ **Keeper of the Lamps** | Gas mains, lamps, printing presses | **Lysander** (since 302 AC) | Varre, who owns the coal the gas is made from |

A Seat lets a house **tax what it runs**. The Governor takes a share of every tax in the city. The Master of the Fleet takes a share of every cargo. The Steward takes a share of every loaf. That's why a Seat matters more than any estate.

**Holding two Seats** is unusual. Antioch holding two, as a house barely sixty years old, is the scandal of the age, and the main topic of conversation at every dinner Antioch isn't invited to.

## 💧 House Antioch

> ***"We Carry."***

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Colours** | Slate blue and silver |
| **Sign** | A silver drop above a crawler's track |
| **Seats** | Governor of Allaneus, Master of the Fleet |
| **Controls** | Ice and water, the ice roads across the Pale, the Icegate and the Haul Road, the melt-houses on the Brink, the Great Pumps, the Skyquay |
| **Seat of the house** | **Tidehouse**, on the eastern Brink of the Well beside the melt-houses. Built of grown blue glass, it looks like ice and is always warm |
| **Head** | **Governor Aldous Antioch** |
| **In the Works** | *"Late, but they come."* |

### The ice-haulers

Every child in the Works knows where Antioch came from, and the Antiochs have never pretended otherwise. They were **ice-haulers**: a family of drivers who took diesel crawlers out across the Pale to the glaciers, cut ice, dragged it home and sold the meltwater to whichever house paid most. They were good at it. They bought more crawlers, then the ice roads, then the melt-houses on the Brink where the ice is turned into water.

Then they did the thing no hauling family had ever done. They lent the Monarch's fleet the money to build twelve new ships, and they asked for a title in return. In **351 AC** the writ came: the Antiochs were a noble house.

The old houses have never forgiven them. Antioch has never cared. The family still keeps the first crawler, *Patience*, in the great hall of Tidehouse, and every heir of the house drives an ice road at least once before they're allowed to sit at the table.

### What Antioch holds

- **The ice roads.** Long tracks across the Pale to the glaciers, from Green to Green along the Seams, kept open by Antioch's crawler crews. Most of the city's water arrives this way. Haulers are the toughest people on Allaneus, and some of the best-paid Works folk alive.
- **The Icegate and the Haul Road.** The ice roads come into the Rim through the Icegate, a pass in the eastern Rimwall, and cross forty kilometres of the Crust on the Haul Road to the Well. It's the one road in the Heights where Works-born crews drive past noble estates, and the estates have never got used to it.
- **The melt-houses.** On the eastern Brink, where the ice is melted down with steam piped up from the Works. Run for Antioch by **House Pell**.
- **The Great Pumps.** As Governor, Antioch keeps the pumping station that drives water down the Well to the districts. More than a hundred districts drink Antioch's water.
- **The Skyquay.** As Master of the Fleet, Antioch keeps the fleet yards high on the southern Rimwall, where the Works builds hulls and the Conservatory grows the ships' hearts. Every cargo that leaves Allaneus or comes down from its moons passes through Antioch's hands, including Varre's ore.
- **The Clean Sky.** The Master of the Fleet answers to the Monarch for every bolt left in orbit, and the Netwrights work to Antioch's orders. It's a duty more than a profit, but it gives Antioch the first look at everything the Nets catch.

### The household

| Who | What they do |
| :--- | :--- |
| 👑 **The family** | The Governor, his sister the Master of the Fleet, their children and cousins. You'll never meet them. If you do, something has gone very wrong, or very right |
| 🎩 **The Stewards** | The senior Liveried who run the house's business. Two silver drops |
| 🧥 **The Liveried** | Antioch's uniformed household: clerks, agents, footmen, factors. One silver drop. Their Livery opens Refined doors and upper gates. Your handler is one of them |
| ⚔️ **The Keel** | Antioch's elite. Blue-black armour, rifles, heavy blades. They end raids, guard the Skyquay, and ride with the ice crawlers. They never speak to the districts they protect |
| 🔧 **The Gaugers** | Antioch's engineers, one in every district pump house. Not quite Liveried, not quite Works. Everyone in the district knows the Gauger, and nobody quite trusts them |
| 🚚 **The haulers** | The crawler crews of the ice roads. Works-born, paid like Terraces folk, loyal to each other first |

### The Livery

A long slate-blue coat with a high white collar and a silver drop at the throat. The coat is Refined: it keeps its wearer warm on the Pale and cool in the Works, and the Weep never marks it. The Livery is how Antioch's people pass the Lockgates and open Refined doors. Wearing it without the right is one of the worst crimes a Works person can commit, and every crew has thought about it.

### The family

- **Governor Aldous Antioch.** Grandson of the first lord. Sixty-odd years old and looks forty. Quiet, careful, and famous for never forgetting a debt in either direction. He governs from Tidehouse and has never been seen below the Terraces.
- **Lady Ilse Antioch.** His younger sister, Master of the Fleet. She drove an ice crawler across the Pale at sixteen for a bet, and runs the Skyquay like the deck of a ship. Haulers love her. The old houses think she's vulgar, and she thinks that's funny.
- **Lord Tobias Antioch.** The Governor's only son and heir. Twenty-four, handsome, popular in the Season, and rumoured to have lost a fortune at cards. The Terraces gossip about him more than anyone else in the Heights.
- **Lady Vesper Antioch.** Ilse's daughter. Nineteen, and already running the ice roads. Haulers call her *the Little Master*.

### How Antioch treats its districts

Better than some, worse than it thinks. Antioch's water is cleaner than most houses', and its pump houses are better kept, because the Governor's own engineers run them. The Keel always come when a district is raided. But Antioch never forgets that the water is a lever, and it isn't shy about pulling it. And the Liveried walk through the Works as if the people in it were furniture.

### What Antioch wants this year

To **keep both Seats** through the Assessment. Every other house wants at least one of them. Antioch needs to look strong, generous and spotless, and it needs its rivals to look weak, greedy and dirty. That's a lot of work, and a lot of it can't be done by people in Livery.

## 🔥 House Varre

> ***"We Endure."***

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Colours** | Black and ember orange |
| **Sign** | A coal burning in a closed fist |
| **Seats** | None, since 381 AC |
| **Controls** | Cinderwell and its coal seams, the fuel-oil refineries, most of the foundry districts, and the Ember Lease: the mines of the red moon |
| **Seat of the house** | **The Ember Court**, on the western Brink of the Well: black glass that glows faintly orange at night, like a coal, or like the moon it's named for |
| **Head** | **Lord Casimir Varre**, the Ember Lord |
| **In the Works** | *"Varre keeps you warm, and keeps the receipt."* |

Varre is one of the seven Charter Houses, and it held the Seat of Master of the Fleet for over a hundred years before Antioch's money took it away in 381 AC. It has wanted it back every day since.

Varre owns what burns. Its coal seams run through the walls of **Cinderwell**, the black well west of the Well, and every big shudder pushes new ones up. Its refineries turn the oil from the deep vents into fuel for crawlers, carts and furnaces. And since 129 AC it has held the **Ember Lease**: the iron and rare-ore mines of the red moon, the richest grant any house on Allaneus holds. The Ember Lord's title comes from the moon, not the coal.

The foundry districts belong to Varre too: Slagside in the Well, the Coils, and Cinderhill and the rest of Cinderwell, joined by the Slag Bore. They're warm, busy, and poor, because Varre pays its foundries in **scrip**, and scrip only buys from Varre's own stores.

The worst of it, for Varre, is the shipping. Every load of ore from the Ember now comes down on Antioch's ships, and Varre pays Antioch for every tonne.

- **Lord Casimir Varre, the Ember Lord.** Lost the Fleet as a young man and has spent thirty years getting ready to take it back. Burn-scarred down one side of his face from a foundry accident as a boy, and refuses to have it grown over. Works folk respect him for that, more than they'd like.
- **The Ember Company.** Varre's crew, and the best-armed crew in the Works. Refined sidearms, good pay, and no sense of humour. If they're on a job, it's against Antioch.
- **The Slagborn.** The gang of Slagside, Varre's biggest district. Tough, proud, and short of water.

**What Varre wants this year:** the Fleet back, and its own ore on its own ships. To get it, Antioch has to look unfit to the Assessor: careless, corrupt or weak. Varre has been collecting Antioch's embarrassments for thirty years. This year it's ready to spend them.

## 🕯️ House Lysander

> ***"Light Shows All."***

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Colours** | White and gold |
| **Sign** | An open eye inside a lamp flame |
| **Seat** | Keeper of the Lamps |
| **Controls** | The gas mains, the lamps of the Span and the Terraces, and every printing press in the city |
| **Seat of the house** | **The Lantern**, a white spire on the northern Brink with a light at its tip that never goes out. On a clear night you can see it from the top of the Rimwall |
| **Head** | **Lady Orla Lysander** |
| **In the Works** | *"If Lysander didn't print it, it didn't happen."* |

Lysander lights the city and tells it what to think. As Keeper of the Lamps it runs the gas that lights the Span, the Crossings, the Galleries and the Terraces, and the presses that print **the Lysander Gazette**, the newssheet every Terraces household reads with breakfast. The Gazette prints the Season's engagements, the Seats' notices, and whichever scandal Lady Orla has decided is true this week.

Lysander's real trade is **knowing things**. Its lamplighters go everywhere, and they talk. Its clerks read everything. Half the house's wealth comes from what it chooses *not* to print.

- **Lady Orla Lysander.** Head of the house, fifty, charming, and never seen without a pair of tinted Refined spectacles that are said to let her read a letter through its envelope.
- **The Lampless.** Lysander's crew. You never see them. You see what they printed about you the next morning.

**What Lysander wants this year:** to decide the Assessment without holding a single extra Seat. Whoever the Gazette says is fit will look fit. Every house is paying Lysander court, and Lysander is enjoying it.

## ⚖️ House Corvane

> ***"It Is Written."***

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Colours** | Grey and iron |
| **Sign** | An open book held shut by a chain |
| **Seat** | Warden of the Well |
| **Controls** | The Greycoats, the Lockgates, the courts, the Drop, the Shardworks |
| **Seat of the house** | **The Register**, a grey stone hall on the southern Brink, where every law, judgement and arrest since the Charter is kept on file |
| **Head** | **Marshal Odile Corvane** |
| **In the Works** | *"Greycoats don't ask. They write it down."* |

Corvane is the oldest of the Charter Houses, and it has held the Seat of Warden of the Well since the day of the Charter. It is the law on Allaneus, in the sense that it decides who gets arrested, who gets tried and who goes to the Drop. Corvane rarely needs to scheme for anything. It just waits for everyone else to break a rule.

The **Greycoats** are Corvane's watch: grey greatcoats, iron-capped batons, and notebooks. They keep the Lockgates, patrol the Terraces and the Span's edges, and come down into the Works only in force. A Greycoat in the Works means someone is about to have a very bad day.

- **Marshal Odile Corvane.** Warden of the Well. Every Greycoat answers to her, and she reads every arrest report herself. She's said to have never once been wrong about a person.
- **The Drop.** Corvane's prison: iron cages hung on chains over the open Well below the Terraces. Prisoners can see the whole city from up there, and can't reach any of it.
- **The Shardworks.** Corvane's penal mine on the Shard, the smallest moon, worked under licence from the Crown since 288 AC. Ten years is the shortest sentence. Prisoners go up on Antioch's ships, which Corvane pays for, and which Antioch never lets it forget. In the Works, being **sent up the Shard** is the one fate worse than the Drop.

**What Corvane wants this year:** order. An Assessor is coming, and the Warden of the Well will be judged by how quiet the city is. Crews make the city noisy. Corvane is quietly offering rewards for anyone who can name the houses behind them.

## 🌾 House Aurel

> ***"From One Grain, Many."***

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Colours** | Wheat gold and green |
| **Sign** | A sheaf of wheat bound with a green ribbon |
| **Seat** | Steward of the Granaries |
| **Controls** | The Sowings (the grain fields of the Crust), the Cradle and its vat-farms, the grain that comes in by ship, and every ration board in the Works |
| **Seat of the house** | **The Granary**, a long low palace on the southern Brink, built over its own grain silos, looking out over the fields |
| **Head** | **Steward Benedek Aurel** |
| **In the Works** | *"Aurel feeds you, and counts the crumbs."* |

Nothing grows on the Pale, so everything the Rim eats comes from Aurel. The **Sowings**, Aurel's grain fields, cover the warm Crust between the Well and the Cradle: wheat from other worlds, growing in soil the vents keep warm, harvested twice a year by Works field gangs on day passes. The **Cradle**, Aurel's own shallow well, has its walls terraced with vat-farms that grow yeast and protein in warm tanks. What the Rim can't grow comes in by ship, and that's where Antioch comes in. Aurel bakes the **grey loaf** and runs the **ration boards** that hand it out. It is the most generous house in the Heights, and the most careful: every slip, every loaf, every district's ration is counted, and a district that falls out of favour finds its slips run out a day early.

- **Steward Benedek Aurel.** A big, warm, laughing man who knows the name of every ration clerk in the city. People like him. People who owe him find that he's less fun.
- **The Gleaners.** A crew who work for whoever pays, mostly Aurel. They always take a little more than the job asked for.

Aurel has one more worry nobody mentions at dinner. In 405 AC the Great Shudder opened a new vent, **the Newmouth**, in the middle of its fields, and the Keepers took it away from Aurel in a day. Aurel was furious, then very quiet, which worried people more.

**What Aurel wants this year:** a third of Aurel's grain still comes in on Antioch's fleet, and Antioch takes a share of every cargo. Aurel would like that to stop. It isn't fussy about whether that means a new Master of the Fleet or just a weaker one.

## 🥀 House Calyx

> ***"All Things Return."***

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Colours** | Deep violet and black |
| **Sign** | A single flower, pressed flat |
| **Seats** | None, for as long as anyone remembers |
| **Controls** | The Hanging Gardens, the perfumeries, and the burial of the Heights' dead |
| **Seat of the house** | **The Hanging Gardens**, terraced gardens on the north-eastern Brink that spill over the edge of the Well |
| **Head** | **The Dowager Calyx** |
| **In the Works** | *"Calyx buries the Heights. Nobody knows who buries Calyx."* |

Calyx is a Charter House, as old as Corvane, and the smallest of the great houses. It holds no Seat, sits on no councils, and gives the best funerals on Allaneus. When a noble dies, Calyx takes the body into the Hanging Gardens, and a year later a new tree is growing there. The house's perfumers make the scents every noble wears in the Season, and its gardeners grow the flowers on every Heights table. Calyx's foresters also keep the ventwood forests of the Crust, and they're the only people in the Heights who go into **the Hollow** without being asked to. Calyx says it's for the wood.

Everyone in the Heights is polite to Calyx, the way people are polite to the undertaker. Nobody is quite sure how it pays for its gardens. Nobody has ever made an enemy of it and kept their good name.

- **The Dowager Calyx.** Head of the house. Nobody knows her first name, or her age. She's been the Dowager since before the Governor was born, and she attends every funeral in the Heights in person.
- **Dried flowers.** Calyx sends a pressed flower from the Gardens to every house in mourning. In the Heights a dried flower means grief, or remembrance, or *"we know what you did"*, depending on the flower.
- **The Quiet Boys.** A crew who wear violet. Nobody will say they work for Calyx. Nobody will say they don't.

**What Calyx wants this year:** nobody knows. Calyx says it wants nothing. That has never stopped anyone in the Heights from watching it closely.

## 🏠 Lesser Houses

Dozens of **lesser houses** live in the Heights too: noble families with a name, an estate and a few districts, but no Seat and no hope of one. Each is **sworn** to a great house, which protects it and expects service in return. Many of them do the great houses' dirty work, and hire crews of their own.

| Lesser house | Sworn to | Known for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **House Pell** | Antioch | Runs the melt-houses for Antioch. Old, proud and poorer than it lets on. Hates being Antioch's servant |
| **House Sallow** | Antioch | Antioch's money-lenders and factors on the Span. Very rich, very quiet |
| **House Ashgrove** | Varre | Runs Varre's refineries. Their people come home smelling of oil and are proud of it |
| **House Quill** | Lysander | Writes half the Gazette. Knows everybody's secrets, sells some of them |
| **House Dunmore** | Corvane | The judges. Every court on Allaneus has a Dunmore on the bench |
| **House Brack** | Aurel | Captains of the grain ships. Tough, off-world, and loyal to whoever pays the harbour fees |
| **House Morrow** | Calyx | Calyx's gardeners and keepers of the funeral roads. Rarely seen at the Season |

Lesser houses change sides more often than great ones. A lesser house that swaps its oath is called **turned**, and a great house that loses one loses face.

## ⚰️ Fallen Houses

There were **seven** Charter Houses. Five are still in the Heights.

- **House Mereth.** Held the water of half the Works after the Dry Year, and treated it badly. Fell in **366 AC** under debts it couldn't pay and a scandal nobody will name. Its Compacts were sold to pay its creditors, and **Antioch bought your district**. Mereth's stamped crown is still on the oldest valves in your pump house. The Mereth estate on the Brink is empty, and the Terraces say it's haunted.
- **House Hollin.** The seventh Charter House. Gone so long that nobody remembers how it ended. Its name is in the Charter. It held its own well, north-west of the Well, and in the 240s AC that well went cold and crusted over in less than a year. Now it's **the Hollow**, a forest nobody builds in, and Hollin's estate isn't on any map. Some Sunk folk claim to be Hollin's descendants. Nobody believes them.

When a house falls, its Seats go to the Monarch's choice, its districts are sold, and its family is never spoken of again in polite company. Every house in the Heights knows it could happen to them. It makes them careful, and it makes them cruel.

## 🗡️ How the Houses Fight

Open war between houses is forbidden, by the Monarch's writ, since the **War of Glass** ended in 236 AC. A house that sends its own soldiers against another house's would lose its Seats and probably its name. So the houses fight another way.

| Weapon | How it's used |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🗡️ **Crews** | Works gangs hired to steal, break, burn and take. Deniable. If caught, they were never hired |
| 🗞️ **Scandal** | A secret, printed in the Gazette at the right moment. Cheaper than a crew, and often worse |
| 💧 **Leverage** | Water, bread, fuel and light. A house can make a rival's districts suffer without anyone firing a shot |
| 💍 **Marriage** | Alliances are made at the Season's weddings, and broken at its funerals |
| ⚖️ **The law** | A rival's crew, caught and handed to Corvane with the right evidence, can sink a whole house |
| 📜 **Writs** | Money sent to the Throne-world, in the hope the next ship brings back the right decision |

**The rules of the game.** Nobody kills a noble. Not directly, and not in the Heights. Nobody harms a Keeper of the Conservatory. Nobody breaks the Margin Peace. And nobody admits to anything, ever. A house that breaks these rules finds every other house against it.

Crews, of course, aren't nobles. Nothing protects them.

## 📯 This Year: the Assessment

Word came down from the Skyquay in the last days of 411 AC: the Monarch is sending an **Assessor**, the first in sixty-four years. Nobody knows when the Assessor's ship will land, how long they'll stay, or what they'll be looking for. Everyone knows what's at stake. Every Seat on Allaneus will be judged.

What that means for the Heights:

- **Antioch** must keep two Seats that every other house wants.
- **Varre** has thirty years of grievance and a chance to be heard.
- **Lysander** will decide what everyone reads about everyone else.
- **Corvane** needs the city quiet, and it isn't.
- **Aurel** wants out from under Antioch's fleet.
- **Calyx** says it wants nothing.

What it means for the Works: **every house wants a crew**. Every house wants its rivals' secrets stolen, its rivals' cargo spoiled, and its own embarrassments buried before the Assessor arrives. There has never been so much work, or so much pay, or so many ways to end up in the Drop.
