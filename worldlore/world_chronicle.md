# 📜 The Chronicle

> **Four hundred and twelve years of Allaneus. The houses wrote it down. The Works remembered it differently.**

Everything on this page is for players. Where the Heights and the Works tell a story differently, both versions are here. Which one is true is for your table to find out, if it ever matters. Back to the [Lore overview](lore.md#-history).

## 🗓️ Counting the Years

Years are counted **AC**, *After Charter*, from the day the Monarch's writ granted Allaneus to the first houses. It's now **412 AC**.

The Works doesn't use the date much. Works folk count years by what happened in them: *"the year of the Grey Cough"*, *"the winter the Kettle flooded"*, *"two years after the Coils burned"*. Ask an old Works woman how old she is and she'll tell you she was born *"three Highwaters after the Mereth crowns came off"*.

A year on Allaneus is a little longer than people say a year is on the Throne-world. Nobody in the Works has any way of checking.

## 📜 The Charter

*0 AC*

**What the houses say.** The Monarch, in wisdom, looked down on Allaneus from the stars and saw the greatest of its green places, the Rim, and knew it for a gift: a fire in the cold that would never go out. The Monarch granted it by writ to the seven most loyal houses of the Ascendancy, **the Charter Houses**: Corvane, Calyx, Aurel, Lysander, Varre, Mereth and Hollin. They came down from the sky on the first ships, landed on the warm Crust beside the greatest of the Rim's living vents, and claimed its edge, **the Brink**.

**What the Works remembers.** Nothing. The Works wasn't there yet.

The Rim was old long before anyone came. Keepers say it's the mouth of a super volcano that last woke before there were people anywhere. Nobody knows what made **the Well**, though. It was already there when the first ships came: a round hole twelve kilometres across, its walls smooth in places as if they'd been cut, dropping into a heat that never fades. The other vents of the Rim are ragged. The Well is round. The houses' histories say it was made by the Ascendancy's first Monarch, long before the Charter. The Works says it was made by the Mother Below, climbing up to see the sky.

## ⛏️ The Descent

*0–40 AC*

The Charter Houses didn't dig the Well. They brought people to do it.

The **Bonded** came on later ships, packed into holds from a dozen worlds of the Ascendancy, each owing their passage and a lifetime of labour to a house. They built the first Hoists and stairs. They cut the Terraces into the upper walls for the houses' servants. Then they went down, and kept going: mining the coal and ore in the walls, laying the first pressure lines, carving the galleries that would one day be the Sunk.

And they dug across. The houses wanted the coal of the western vent and the warm walls of the southern one, so the Bonded drove **bores** through the rock to reach them, each at the depth of the work it served. The first bore reached Cinderwell in 27 AC. By the Unbinding there were bores at every layer, and the Rim had become one city with many holes in it.

They lived where they worked, in cave-rooms cut into the walls, and they died there too. The Bonded were the first people to call the Well home, because they had nowhere else to call it.

> **What the Works remembers.** Every Works family claims to know which world its Bonded came from, and none of them agree. Old songs mention places nobody can find on any map: *Thessaly Deep*, *the Saltreach*, *Coram*. The songs say the Bonded were promised land of their own when their bond was paid. Nobody's bond ever was.

## 🔥 The Kindling

*41 AC*

In 41 AC the Bonded reached the floor of the Well, two kilometres down: a crust of old cooled rock, laid over the vent like a lid, with a ragged hole in the middle where the vent still breathed. They called the hole **the Throat**, and they found the heat.

The first deep boilers were built right round it: great iron vessels sunk into the floor at the Throat's edge, where water fed in from above came up as steam hot enough to drive anything. They called it **the Kindling**. Within ten years steam was running up the walls of the Well in iron pipes to the Brink, lifting the Hoists, warming the houses, pumping water, and driving the foundries that built more pipes.

The Kindling has never once gone cold, except twice: once by accident, during the Long Shift, and once on purpose, every Banking Night.

> **What the Works remembers.** The Kindling cost lives from the first day. The heat at the bottom kills anyone who stays too long without water and rest, and the first boilers burst often. The Bonded who worked the Kindling were the first to speak of **the Mother Below**: the heat as a person, generous and dangerous, who keeps you warm and takes her share. The first prayers on Allaneus were said at the Kindling. They were mostly apologies.

## 💎 The First Refinement

*Around 60 AC*

**What the houses say.** As the city grew, the Monarch sent the Charter Houses a second gift: **seed-stock** from the Throne-world, the beginning of all Refinement, and Keepers sworn to tend it. The Crown raised **the Conservatory** on the Crust beside the Well to hold it, over a small vent of its own, and the first Refined things grown on Allaneus were the houses' own doors, so that their homes would know them.

**What the Works remembers.** The story of **the Gardener**: a Bonded woman who found a strange seed in the warm rock at the edge of the Throat, among the breathmoss, and grew a coat for her son so that the Kindling wouldn't burn him. The houses heard about it, came down for the first time, and took the seed, the coat and the Gardener up to the Heights. She never came back. But she left a cutting hidden somewhere in the Works, and one day it will flower.

The Keepers say the story is nonsense. They say it with a sort of practised patience, as if they've had to say it many, many times.

> **What everyone agrees on.** From around 60 AC, Refinement belonged to the Heights. The first laws against Works folk carrying it were written the same year the Conservatory opened.

## ⛓️ The Unbinding

*112 AC*

In 112 AC a writ came from the Throne-world: **the Bonded were free.** Every debt was cancelled. Every family could go where it liked.

There was nowhere to go. The ships to other worlds cost more than a Bonded family would see in a lifetime, and outside the Rim was the Pale, where the air runs thin and the cold kills in an hour. A few families tried anyway, walking out along the Seams to the wild Greens. Nobody heard from them again, or nobody upstairs did. So the Bonded stayed, and went on doing the same work, for wages now instead of debt. The houses discovered that wages were cheaper than keeping people alive.

The hungry years after the Unbinding were the worst the Works has known, until the Dry Year. Nobody was obliged to feed the Bonded any more. The houses stopped.

### The pump clubs

What the Works did instead was look after itself. Neighbours took turns guarding the **pump house**, the one place in each street where the house's water came in, so that nobody could steal it, waste it or sell it. They shared out the water fairly, then the food, then the work. These were **the pump clubs**, and every gang in the Works today grew out of one.

The pump clubs gave the Works its first leaders (the **Keyholders**, who kept the key to the pump-house door), its first marks (chalked on the pump house to show whose it was) and its first oath, still sworn by every gang member in the Works:

> *"Water for all, or water for none."*

## 🔴 The Ember Lease

*129 AC*

From the Crust, three moons are always somewhere in the sky: the great white **Salt**, the small fast **Shard**, and the rust-red **Ember**. The Salt had been the Crown's since the Charter, mined for the metals the Mint and the Conservatory need. In 129 AC the Monarch granted the Ember too, by writ, to **House Varre**: the **Ember Lease**, the right to mine the red moon's iron and rare ores for as long as Varre stays loyal.

Varre held the Fleet then, so Varre's own ships carried its own ore down to its own foundries, and for two hundred and fifty years nobody on Allaneus burned brighter. The Ember Lord's title comes from the Lease, not from the coal.

> **What the Works remembers.** The first miners sent up to the Ember were volunteers from Varre's foundry districts, promised Terraces wages and a Terraces flat when they came home. Some came home. Varre kept the promise to those who did, which is more than most houses would have done, and the foundry districts have been Varre's ever since.

Since **381 AC**, when Antioch took the Fleet, every load of Varre's ore has come down on Antioch's ships, and Varre has paid Antioch for the carrying. The Ember Lord looks up at the red moon every night and thinks about that.

## 🪟 The War of Glass

*231–236 AC*

The only open war between the houses of Allaneus.

It began with a Seat. The Governor of the time, from House Hollin, died with no heir, and four houses claimed the Governorship at once. When the Monarch's writ was slow in coming, they stopped waiting. For five years, house soldiers fought across the Crust and through the Galleries, and the houses drafted whole Works districts to fight for them in the Runs. Refined weapons were used openly for the only time in the city's history. The great glasshouses of the Heights were shattered one by one, which is how the war got its name.

It ended in 236 AC, when a writ arrived from the Throne-world. It named Varre's candidate as Governor, and it forbade any house of Allaneus to make war on another, on pain of losing its Seats and its name forever.

> **What the houses learned.** Never fight in the open. Since the War of Glass, the houses fight with scandal, leverage, marriage and the law, and with **crews** hired from the Works, who can be disowned if they're caught.

> **What the Works learned.** Nobody counted the Works dead from the War of Glass. The houses' histories list the noble dead by name: forty-one of them. The Works lost whole districts. *"Glass for them, blood for us"* is still said in the Works when the houses fall out.

## 🌲 The Closing of the Hollow

*Around 244 AC*

House Hollin had its own well: a vent north-west of the Well, smaller and quieter, with Hollin's estate on its edge and Hollin's districts down its walls. In the years after the War of Glass, that well **went cold**.

Vents do close. The Crust moves, and now and then a small vent stops breathing and crusts over. But they close slowly, over lifetimes. Hollin's closed in less than a year. The breathmoss in its throat went grey. The glasswings stopped flying over it. The steam thinned, then stopped, and the walls began to frost. The Galleries and Runs into it were bricked up, and the people in it were moved out, or weren't.

House Hollin disappears from the histories soon after. Nobody seems to know exactly when, or why. Hollin's well is called **the Hollow** now. A ventwood forest grows on top of it, which nobody planted, and nobody builds there.

> **What the houses say.** A natural closing, and a tragedy. The two events are unrelated.

> **What the Works remembers.** That the Hollow's districts were the last to leave, and that some of them never came out through the Runs at all. Sunk folk say they went down instead, through the old bores, and that their children are still down there.

## 🕰️ The Quiet Century

*237–317 AC*

Eighty years without a war, and the years the city grew into what it is now.

- The **Span** was built round the walls of the Well, and the **Margin Peace** agreed, so that the houses had somewhere to trade without being seen to. The **Crossings** were widened to join it to every other well, and the Peace was stretched to cover them.
- The **Runs** got their steam rail, and coal, grain and people began moving between the wells' Works faster than they ever had.
- **Corvane** opened the **Shardworks**, a penal mine on the small moon, in 288 AC. The Drop had been the only prison. Now there was somewhere further to send people.
- **House Lysander** won the new Seat of **Keeper of the Lamps** in 302 AC, lit the Span with gas, and started the **Gazette**.
- The old mines under the floor flooded with **bitterwater** when the deep pumps were abandoned, and **the Sunk** was born. Smugglers moved in within a year.
- **Varre** held the Fleet, **Mereth** held most of the Works' water, and both grew fat.
- The Works grew faster than anyone upstairs noticed. The districts doubled, then doubled again, and the pump houses that fed them stayed the same size.

> **What the Works remembers.** The Quiet Century wasn't quiet below the Span. Water was short and getting shorter. Mereth raised its water price nine times in forty years. The pump clubs had become gangs, and the gangs had started fighting each other over pressure. By 317 AC, every district knew something was going to break.

## ✊ The Long Shift

*318 AC*

It started at the Kindling.

In the spring of 318 AC, a boiler burst in the deep floor and killed eleven stokers. It was the third that year. House Mereth, which owned the Kindling's water, sent a notice that the stokers' families would be docked the cost of the repairs.

The stokers walked off the floor. Then the fitters walked off the lines. Then the foundries banked their fires. For the first time since 41 AC, **the Kindling went cold**.

Without steam, the Hoists stopped. The pumps stopped. The lamps on the Span went dark. The Heights, for the first time, felt cold. The Works called it **the Long Shift**, because everyone was on it, and nobody was working.

For forty days, the Works held out. The gangs held their streets. The pump clubs shared what water they had left. The Works asked for three things: **fair water, a fair wage, and a fair hearing at the Seat Hall.**

---

## 🚱 The Dry Year

*318–319 AC*

The houses' answer was to **stop the water**.

Every house that held a Works district shut its valves. With the Kindling cold, no water came up from the floor either. The Works had what was in its tanks and nothing more.

The Long Shift lasted forty days. The Dry Year lasted most of a year, because even after the Works gave in, the houses kept the valves shut until every district had **signed**.

Nobody knows how many died. The Works says a third of everyone. The houses' histories say *"some thousands"*. Whole districts emptied and were never filled again. The ones that survived did so by drinking the Weep, and boiling bitterwater, and burying their dead in the furnaces because there was no water to spare for anything else. That's when the Works started sending its dead **up the stack**.

### Kett

The best-known story of the Dry Year is **Kett**, a line fitter from a district nobody agrees on. When her district's children started dying, Kett climbed the outside of Mereth's main pressure line, sixty metres up a sweating iron pipe, to the shut-off valve, and opened it by hand. The water ran for an hour before Mereth's guards reached her. They shut the valve and threw her off the line.

Kett is the first **Line Saint**. Her mark, a valve wheel with one spoke missing, is chalked on pump houses all over the Works, and every fitter touches it before a climb.

### The Water Compacts

The Dry Year ended with **the Water Compacts**. Each district signed, through its gang, a Compact with one house:

- The house would send the district water, and protect it from raiders.
- The district would work for the house, stay loyal to it alone, and do whatever the house asked of it.
- The Compact would last **as long as the water runs**.

Nobody signed freely. A district that didn't sign stayed dry. Within a year, every surviving district in the Works belonged to a house, and the gangs had become what they are now: the keepers of a contract.

> **What the houses say.** The Compacts brought order and safety to a lawless Works, and no district has gone dry since.

> **What the Works says.** *"Dry year on you"* is still the worst curse in the Works.

## ⚓ The Rise of Antioch

*320–365 AC*

The Dry Year taught every house that water was power. One family of commoners had already worked that out.

The **Antiochs** were ice-haulers: crawler-drivers who crossed the Pale to the glaciers, cut ice and dragged it home. During the Dry Year, while the houses kept their own valves shut, the Antiochs kept hauling, bringing their crawlers in through the Icegate and across the Crust on what's now called the Haul Road, and sold their ice to anyone who'd pay, including Works districts with nothing but chips hidden under the floor. Some in the Works remember them kindly for that. Others remember the price.

Afterwards, the Antiochs were rich. They bought more crawlers, then the ice roads, then the melt-houses on the Brink. By 340 AC they were the biggest suppliers of water to the houses themselves.

- **348 AC.** The last **Assessor** came to Allaneus. Nobody knows what the Assessor and the Antiochs said to each other. Everyone knows what happened next.
- **351 AC.** The Antiochs lent the Monarch's fleet the money to build twelve new ships, and a writ came from the Throne-world: the Antiochs were a **noble house**, the first new house on Allaneus since the Charter.

The Charter Houses closed their doors to them. Antioch didn't knock.

## 🥀 The Fall of Mereth

*366 AC*

House Mereth had held more of the Works' water than any other house since the Compacts. In 366 AC, it fell.

What actually happened depends who you ask. The Gazette of the time spoke of *"debts beyond any house's means"* and *"a matter of grave impropriety"*, and printed nothing more. The Terraces said a Mereth heir had been caught trading in seed-stock. The Works said the Mother Below had finally collected on the Dry Year.

Whatever it was, Mereth lost everything. Its estate on the Brink was shut. Its family vanished from the Season. And its Water Compacts, the water of more than a hundred districts, were sold to pay its creditors.

**Antioch bought most of them.** Including yours.

That's why the oldest valves in your pump house carry Mereth's stamped crown, filed half off. When Antioch's Gaugers came to take over, they didn't replace the old valves. They just defaced them. Works folk still call anything old, broken and still in use **a Mereth crown**.

## ❓ Questions Nobody Can Answer

Things everyone in the Works has wondered about. If anyone knows the answers, they aren't telling.

- **What made the Well?** It was here before the Charter. Every other vent in the Rim is ragged, and the Well is round. Its walls are too smooth in places. Something is at the bottom, and it's warm.
- **Was there a Gardener?** The Keepers say no. The Works says yes. Nobody has ever found the cutting.
- **Where did seed-stock come from?** The Keepers say the Throne-world. But Refined things are warm and damp and busy, just like breathmoss, and the Conservatory was built over a vent of its own. Wheat and roses from other worlds grow fine in a glasshouse. Why would Refinement need a vent?
- **What happened to House Hollin?** A Charter House doesn't just disappear. This one did, at the same time its well went cold, and nobody upstairs will say how.
- **What's the Newmouth?** A new vent, in Aurel's fields, that the Keepers wanted badly enough to take in a day.
- **Is the Mother waking?** The Rim is a volcano. The shudders have come closer together every year since 405 AC. The Keepers' listening posts hear everything, and the Keepers say nothing.
- **What did Mereth do?** Something bad enough to end a Charter House. The Gazette never printed it.
- **What did the last Assessor see?** In 348 AC an Assessor came. Three years later, ice-haulers were nobles.
- **Who is the Monarch?** The face on the chips has never changed in four hundred years.
- **What's in the deep Sunk?** Below the lamps, the galleries go down further than the Bonded ever dug, into the throat of the vent. Sometimes things come up.
- **What do the Nets catch?** Mostly rubbish. But now and then an auction at the Skyquay is cancelled, and the catch goes somewhere else.
