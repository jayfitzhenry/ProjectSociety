## 🏭 The Works

The Works fills the bottom two-thirds of the Well: the lower walls, stacked with tenements and foundries in ledges and galleries, and the floor, a crowded plain of boiler halls, slag heaps, rail yards and streets. Above it all hangs **the Murk**, the ceiling of steam that starts just below the Span. Furnace light turns its underside orange. Nobody in the Works sees the sun unless they climb.

It's divided into **districts**, hundreds of them, each a few streets around a pump house, each with a gang and a house. District borders are older than any map. They're marked by gang colours tied to the pipes, by painted valve wheels, and by where the pressure changes: step over the line and the water tastes different.

### The three Works

People talk about the Works as if it were one place. It's really three.

| Part | Where | What it's like |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🧱 **The Walls** | The ledges and galleries cut into the lower walls of the Well | Tenements stacked ten high, linked by iron stairs and rope bridges. Cooler, cleaner, and a long way to fall. The better-off Works districts are up here, near the Span |
| 🏭 **The Floor** | The bottom of the Well | Foundries, rail yards, boiler halls, slag. Hot, loud and crowded. Most districts are down here |
| 🔥 **The Deep Floor** | The centre of the floor, around the Kindling | So hot the Weep turns back to steam before it lands. Only stokers live here, and not for long |

## ⚒️ What the Works Makes

Everything the Heights won't grow. Every trade has its own district, its own pride, and its own way of getting killed.

| Trade | What they do | People say |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🔥 **Stokers** | Feed and tend the Kindling's deep boilers | *"Stokers don't get old. They get done."* |
| 🔧 **Line fitters** | Keep the pressure lines running, crawling through ducts and up pipe stacks | Fitters climb anything. Every thief wants to be a fitter, and most fitters are thieves |
| 🫕 **Foundry hands** | Pour brass and iron, haul slag | Hands like boot leather. Mostly Varre's people |
| ⛏️ **Seam-cutters** | Mine coal from the deep walls | Varre's again. They come up black and they come up coughing |
| 🛠️ **Yard crews** | Build hulls and crawler frames for the Skyquay and the ice roads | Antioch's. The best-paid trade in the Works, and they know it |
| 🧵 **Mill hands** | Spin and weave cloth in the steam mills | Mostly women and kids. The mills never stop |
| 🧱 **Brickers** | Fire bricks from Well-clay | Every building in the Works is theirs |
| 🚂 **Carters** | Drive steam carts and freight crawlers between districts | Know every checkpoint and every shortcut |
| 🍞 **Vat-tenders** | Tend Aurel's vat-farms under the rim | The only Works folk who work near daylight. They come home smelling of yeast |
| 🕯️ **Lamplighters** | Light and mend the gas lamps on the Span | Lysander's. They hear everything, and they're paid to |

**Who it's all for.** The yards build the ships that carry Antioch's cargo off the world. The foundries make the pipes that carry the water. The mills make the cloth the Terraces wear. And the Kindling drives all of it. When the Heights says the city runs itself, it means the Works runs it.

## 🔔 A Day Under the Murk

There's no sunrise in the Works, so the Works runs on **bells**.

| Bell | What happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🔔 **First bell** | The day shift starts. Twelve hours. Kids go to the Hall, or to the mills |
| 📯 **The hooter** | Every district's biggest works sounds the hour. People tell the time by counting hooters |
| 🍲 **Half bell** | A short break. Pump tea and fried dough off a boiler-top |
| 🔔 **Second bell** | The night shift starts. The day shift goes home, eats, drinks, and sleeps in the beds the night shift just left |
| 🌑 **Slack Day** | One day in ten. The boilers run low, the shifts rest, and the district comes out into the street |

People talk in bells: *"two bells ago"* is this morning, *"back by first bell"* is tomorrow.

### Slack Day

The one day nobody works, if they can help it. People sleep late, then:

- go to the **Hall** to hear the ration board argue, and to sing;
- go to **the Kettle**, or their own district's pit, for the fights;
- climb to the roofs to race **pigeons**, which fly up through the Murk towards the light and always come home;
- play **Bones**, the Works card game, which every district plays with different rules;
- **court**, mostly on the iron stairs, mostly badly;
- and visit the dead's names, chalked on the wall of the district furnace.

### Weather

It's always warm and always wet. The **Weep** falls out of the Murk day and night: fine, black, warm drizzle that marks every collar by half bell. Near the Deep Floor it's hot enough to scald. On the Walls it's cool. When the Pale outside has a hard frost, the Murk drops lower and the Works gets darker for a week. People call that **a low sky**, and tempers get short.

## 🏠 Home and Family

Most Works families rent **two rooms** in a tenement: one to sleep in, one to cook, wash, work and argue in. Rent is about 4c a month, paid to the gang or to whoever the gang says. Water comes from the tap on the landing, if the district's lucky, or from the pump house, if it isn't.

- **Family** is wide. Cousins, neighbours, the old woman from three doors down who minded you when your mother was on shift: all family. Works folk don't use surnames. You're *Dace of Cistern Row*, or *Merren's girl*, or *the fitter with the bad hand*.
- **Kids** work from about ten, in the mills or as runners. In a district that's doing well, they go to the **Hall** first, to learn their letters and their numbers. A Works kid who can read is worth twice as much and gets cheated half as often.
- **The old** are rare and respected. Anyone who's lived through forty years of the Works knows things. Every district has an **elder** whose word counts for more than the ration board's.
- **Marriage** is sharing a ration book. Two people go to the ration board together, ask for one book, and the board writes both names in it. The district throws a party. Breaking up is getting two books again, and the district throws a different kind of party.
- **Sickness** is the thing everyone fears. The **clinic** is usually a corner of the pump house, run by whoever knows the most and charges the least. Street doctors charge more. Upstairs doctors cost more than a family earns in a year, and report what they find.

## 💧 The Pump House

The heart of every district. It's where the house's water arrives from the Great Pumps, where it's shared out, and where the district's life happens.

- **The gauge.** A great brass pressure gauge hangs over the pump-house door where everyone can see it. When the needle drops, the whole street goes quiet.
- **The Gauger.** Every pump house has a **Gauger**, the house's engineer, who keeps the valves and answers to the house. Not quite Liveried, not quite Works. Gaugers live in the district and drink in its bars, and everybody knows they write reports.
- **The Keyholder's door.** The gang holds the key to the pump house's side door. The Gauger has the valves. The gang has the door. That arrangement is older than the Compacts, and both sides respect it.
- **The wall of names.** In most districts, the dead's names are chalked on the pump-house wall, or on the district furnace, beside Kett's mark.
- **The clinic** and the **Hall** are often built against it, because that's where the warm water is.

When the crew send chips home, this is usually where they go.

## 🥊 Gangs and Rivals

### What a gang is

Every district has one gang. It keeps order, settles arguments, collects rent and dues, guards the pump house, and holds the district's contract with its house. It's also usually the biggest employer in the district after the house itself. A gang is part protection, part family, part business, and it can't be any of those without the other two.

| Who | What they do |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🗝️ **The Keyholder** | The gang boss. Holds the key to the pump-house door, and the contract |
| 👥 **The crew** | The handful who do the house's deniable work. That's you |
| 💪 **The muscle** | Guards, collectors and the Watch. Most gang members, most of the time |
| 🏃 **Runners** | Kids who carry messages, keep lookout and learn the trade |
| 🧓 **The elders** | Old members who don't work any more. They advise, and everyone listens, eventually |

**The oath.** Every gang member swears the pump clubs' oath, at the pump house, with a hand on the valve: *"Water for all, or water for none."* Breaking it is the worst thing a gang member can do. A traitor is **shut out**: their name is scrubbed from the wall, and no door in the district opens for them again.

**Colours and marks.** Every gang has a colour (a rag tied round the arm, or round the pipes at the edges of its streets) and a mark, chalked on walls and painted on valve wheels. Gang marks are the oldest writing in the Works, and every Streetwise kid can read them: *safe house*, *Greycoats about*, *this one pays*, *this one talks*.

### Rival gangs

| Gang | District | House | What you need to know |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🔥 **The Slagborn** | Slagside | Varre | The biggest gang in the Works. Burn-scarred and proud. Their water is bad and they know where the good water is |
| 🌀 **The Coilers** | The Coils | Varre | Old Hosk's gang. Respected everywhere. They'd rather trade than fight, but they can fight |
| 🌾 **The Sheaves** | Tallow Steps | Aurel | Well-fed, by Works standards. Aurel looks after them, and they look down on everyone else |
| 🕯️ **The Wicks** | Brasswater | Lysander | Lamplighters' gang, up on the Walls near the Span. Sell information to anyone |
| ⚖️ **The Ninefold** | Ninefold | Corvane | The only gang that Greycoats leave alone. Everyone wonders what it costs them |
| 🪦 **Low Mereth** | Low Mereth | None | The last district that never signed with anyone after Mereth fell. Dry, poor, and still there. Nobody understands how |
| 🐀 **The Underlings** | None | None | Raiders living in the dry upper galleries of the Sunk. More of them every year. They hit Antioch's districts hardest |

### Rival crews

Every house has crews, and the good ones have names.

| Crew | Works for | What you need to know |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🗡️ **The Ember Company** | Varre | The best-armed crew in the Works. Refined sidearms. If they're on a job, it's against Antioch |
| 🕯️ **The Lampless** | Lysander | Spies and thieves of paper. You never see them, you just read about yourself |
| 🌾 **The Gleaners** | Mostly Aurel | Work for anyone who pays. Always take a little more than the job asked |
| 🪦 **The Quiet Boys** | Nobody will say | They wear violet. The people they visit don't talk afterwards |
| ⚓ **The Deadweight** | Antioch | Another Antioch crew, from the yard districts. Older, bigger and better paid than yours. They don't like competition |
| 🦈 **Tallis's Teeth** | Mother Tallis | Not a house crew. Debt collectors from the Sunk, and very good at it |

> 🗡️ **Crews know each other.** The Works is big, but the business is small. Rival crews drink in the same Margin bars and use the same fences. Most of the time they're polite. A crew that kills another crew's member starts something that won't end quickly.

## ⚖️ The Law Below the Span

Officially, the law is House Corvane's, and the **Greycoats** enforce it. In practice, Greycoats rarely come below the Span except in force: thirty of them, with carbines, to make an arrest or put down a riot. When they come, the street empties.

The rest of the time, the law in the Works is **the gang's**.

- **Theft** inside the district means paying it back, twice. Theft from another district is the other gang's business, and might start a war.
- **Violence** between neighbours is settled by the Keyholder. Violence that kills someone is settled by the elders, and the answer is usually that someone leaves the district for good.
- **Talking to Greycoats** about gang business is the one crime nobody forgives.
- **Debts** are always paid. The gang pays them if it has to, and then you owe the gang.

**Refined gear** is the one thing gangs and Greycoats agree on: being caught with it brings Greycoats down on the whole district. Most Keyholders want to know if their crew's carrying polish, so they can be somewhere else when the Greycoats come.

## 🤝 The Margin

The Margin is two things: a place, **the Span**, and a way of doing business that happens wherever the upper and lower layers meet.

### The Span

A ring of iron bridges, galleries and market halls, hung across the Well about a third of the way down, on chains and girders anchored in the walls. It's half a mile wide in places and three miles round. The top of the Murk laps at its underside, so the lower galleries are wet with the Weep, and the upper galleries see a little grey daylight from above. Gas lamps burn on every corner, day and night: Lysander's.

The Thousand Steps reach it from below. The Hoists stop at it on their way up. Everyone can get to the Span, which is the point.

| Place on the Span | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🏛️ **The Glass Market** | The biggest market hall in the city. Terraces goods, Works goods, off-world goods. Everything legal, mostly |
| 🍺 **The Halfway House** | A tavern built where the north bridges meet. Every fixer in the Margin drinks here, and the back room is where the Brokers' Table sits |
| 🪝 **Hook Street** | The lowest gallery, wet with the Weep. Cheap bars, cheap rooms, Specialist dealers, and the Margin's doctors who don't ask questions |
| 📜 **The Exchange** | Where the houses' factors trade cargo, grain and water shares. Terraces only, in theory |
| 🌉 **The Long Bridge** | The only bridge that crosses the whole Well. Duels are fought on it at dawn, by people who think dawn matters |

### The Margin Peace

The houses agreed it after the War of Glass, and it's held for nearly two hundred years:

1. **No violence on the Span.** No blades drawn, no shots fired, no one thrown off.
2. **No arrests on the Span.** Greycoats can walk the Span. They can't take anyone off it.
3. **No house business in the open.** Liveried come to the Span out of Livery, and everybody pretends not to recognise them.

Nobody enforces the Peace with soldiers. The **Brokers' Table**, the oldest fixers on the Span, enforces it with something worse: if you break the Peace, every fixer, dealer and doorkeeper in the Margin stops doing business with you, forever. Crews that have broken the Peace don't last long. They can't sell anything, buy anything or meet anyone.

### Fixers

Fixers find buyers, make introductions, carry messages, arrange meetings and vouch for people who'd never otherwise be in the same room. They take a cut of everything. Good fixers are friendly with every house and loyal to none. The best ones are Fallen Servants who still know which fork to use.

## 🛶 The Sunk

Under the Works floor are the mines the Bonded dug, and the galleries they lived in, and the pump halls that once kept the water out. When the deep pumps were abandoned in the Quiet Century, the **bitterwater** came up: warm, salty, faintly glowing in places, and poisonous to drink. It filled the lower galleries completely, and the upper ones up to the waist. That's the Sunk.

### Getting around

On foot, along ledges and old gallery floors. By rope and ladder, between levels. And by **barge**: long flat boats poled or chugged along the flooded galleries by **ferrymen**, who know the channels and charge for it. You don't travel the Sunk without a ferryman, unless you want to drown or be found by the wrong people.

### Who's down there

Nobody rules the Sunk. Power is shared, uneasily, between a few kinds of people:

| Who | What they do |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🛶 **The ferrymen** | Know the channels, carry anyone and anything, and never say who they've carried. Every ferryman pays the others' tolls. Break their rules and no boat will take you |
| 🦈 **The lenders** | Lend chips to gangs and districts that can't get them anywhere else. The debt always comes back doubled. The biggest is **Mother Tallis** |
| 🏷️ **The fences** | Buy and sell what can't be traded upstairs: stolen goods, forged papers, and **Refinement**. The only place in the city to buy polish |
| 🧹 **The cleaners** | Make problems disappear: witnesses, evidence, the occasional body. Expensive, quick and very quiet |
| 🐀 **The squatters** | People with nowhere else. Raiders like the Underlings, runaways, the shut out, the sick. The Sunk takes everyone |

### Places in the Sunk

| Place | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🛶 **Gutterlight** | A floating market of lashed-together barges in the biggest flooded hall, lit by lamps hung from the ceiling. Where the fences trade Refinement |
| ⛪ **The Drowned Chapel** | A Bonded hall, half underwater. The lenders meet here, and nobody draws a blade inside |
| 🚪 **Mereth's Gate** | A great iron door in the deep galleries, stamped with Mereth's crown and welded shut. Nobody knows what Mereth kept down there |
| 🌀 **The Sump** | The lowest open water. The ferrymen won't go past it |

### The rules of the Sunk

1. **Pay the ferry.** Always, and before you get on.
2. **Keep to the lamps.** A lamp means somebody's watching the channel. No lamp means nobody is.
3. **Don't go deeper than the Sump.** The galleries go down further than the Bonded ever dug. People who go looking don't come back, or come back wrong.
4. **Don't drink the water.** Obviously.

**Is it dangerous?** For a crew that pays, keeps to the lamps and minds its manners, the Sunk is mostly **expensive**. For a crew that owes a lender, it's dangerous. For a crew that crosses a ferryman, it's fatal.

## 🧊 The Pale and the Haulers

Outside the Well is **the Pale**: frozen stone plains and glaciers, hundreds of miles of them, under a white sky. The wind never stops, and the cold kills in an hour. Nothing grows. Nothing lives out there.

Nothing, except the **ice-haulers**: Antioch's crawler crews, who drive great diesel crawlers along the **ice roads** to the glaciers, cut ice, and drag it back to the melt-houses on the rim. A run takes weeks. Crawlers break down, and the crew has to fix them in the open. Some don't come back. Haulers are Works-born, paid like Terraces clerks, and loyal to each other before anything else, Antioch included.

Most Works folk will never see the Pale. But every Works family knows where its water comes from, and every crew that does a job for Antioch is, in the end, working for the ice.
